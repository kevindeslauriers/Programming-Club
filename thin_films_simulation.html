📚 Physics Concepts

Thin film interference occurs when light waves reflect off the top and bottom surfaces of a thin film, creating interference patterns. This phenomenon is responsible for the colorful patterns seen in soap bubbles, oil slicks, and anti-reflective coatings.

When light strikes a thin film:

  • Some light reflects off the top surface
  • Some light enters the film and reflects off the bottom surface
  • The two reflected waves interfere with each other
  • Constructive interference creates bright colors
  • Destructive interference creates dark regions

Key Equations

Path Difference: Δ = 2nt cos(θ₂)
Snell's Law: n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)

✨ Constructive Interference

2nt cos(θ₂) = (m + ½)λ

Bright reflection (when one phase shift occurs)

🌑 Destructive Interference

2nt cos(θ₂) = mλ

Dark reflection (when one phase shift occurs)

Where: m = 0, 1, 2, ... (order number), n = refractive index, t = thickness, θ₂ = angle in film

Phase Shift Rules

A 180° phase shift (π radians) occurs when light reflects from a medium with a higher refractive index.

n₁ < n₂

Phase shift occurs at top surface reflection

n₁ > n₂

No phase shift at top surface reflection

n₂ < n₃

Phase shift occurs at bottom surface reflection

n₂ > n₃

No phase shift at bottom surface reflection

Important Cases

  • One phase shift: Conditions reverse (constructive ↔ destructive)
  • Zero or two phase shifts: Standard conditions apply

Real-World Applications

🫧 Soap Bubbles

Colorful patterns from varying film thickness creating different interference conditions for different wavelengths.

🛢️ Oil Slicks

Rainbow patterns on water surfaces from thin oil films with thickness comparable to light wavelengths.

📷 Anti-Reflective Coatings

Engineered thin films on lenses to cause destructive interference of reflected light, reducing glare.

🪞 Optical Filters

Precise thin film stacks used to selectively transmit or reflect specific wavelengths.
